02 — The Problem Overview
Critical Emergency Gap • 4 Core Pain Points
Emergency Response in the Philippines:
4 Critical Pain Points Threatening Citizen Survival
Emergency information needs to reach responders quickly, accurately, and with usable location information. In crisis conditions, traditional communication channels break down due to panic, network loss, and physical danger. D1ALERT is engineered to resolve these 4 foundational pain points, eliminating delays in the alert-to-dispatch process across the Philippines.

03 — Problem Deep-Dive & Operational Gaps
Detailed Breakdown • Root Cause Analysis
Why Existing Emergency Channels Fail:
In-Depth Analysis of Core Operational Obstacles
Traditional emergency reporting assumes a calm caller, an active internet or voice connection, and open ability to speak. In real-world crises across the Philippines, these assumptions routinely break down. Below is the in-depth operational breakdown of each critical challenge and why structural intervention is required.
01
Delayed Emergency Reporting
  • Citizens may be panicked, injured, unable to speak, or unable to clearly explain what is happening.
  • Dispatchers may need additional time to identify the emergency, determine the appropriate responder, and locate the incident.
  • This is especially critical during fires, accidents, medical emergencies, crimes, and disasters.
02
Dependence on Internet-Based Communication
  • Many emergency applications depend on mobile data, Wi-Fi, or cloud services.
  • Internet connectivity can become unreliable during typhoons, floods, earthquakes, power interruptions, and network congestion.
  • Only 48.8% of Philippine households had internet access at home in 2024, according to the PSA cited in the concept note.
03
Difficulty Providing an Exact Location
  • During an emergency, a person may not know their exact address or may be too distressed to communicate it.
  • This can make it harder for responders to identify where assistance is needed.
04
Difficulty Reporting During Silent or High-Risk Emergencies
  • Victims of assault, robbery, stalking, or other active threats may be unable to safely speak.
  • Traditional voice-based reporting can be difficult or dangerous in these situations.
Existing emergency communication methods can depend heavily on voice communication, internet connectivity, or manual reporting.

04 — The Solution
System Architecture
D1ALERT — Offline-First Emergency Alerts.
One Button. No Internet Required.
D1ALERT SYSTEM is an offline-first emergency alert system combining on-device AI (YAMNet acoustic detection, Vosk offline speech transcription, and MLP classifier), discreet physical-button confirmation, location fallback, and low-bandwidth SMS alerts sent directly to authorized emergency organizations for human dispatcher validation.

Alert Pipeline — 7 Steps
01
Step 01 • Activation
Physical button pressed — Discreet, requires no speaking. Designed for victims of assault, stalking, or active threats who cannot safely vocalize.
02
Step 02 • Acoustic Detection
YAMNet detects emergency audio events locally on-device. No API call, no internet connection required at any point.
03
Step 03 • Speech Recognition
Vosk performs offline speech-to-text transcription — captures any verbal context the user is able to provide.
04
Step 04 • Classification
MLP Classifier determines the preliminary emergency type (fire, medical, crime, disaster, etc.) entirely on-device with no cloud dependency.
05
Step 05 • Location Fallback
Best-available location obtained via GPS fallback system. Works even in low-signal and no-data environments.
06
Step 06 • SMS Gateway
Structured SMS containing emergency type, location coordinates, and device ID is sent to the authorized emergency organization dispatcher portal.
07
Step 07 • Human Dispatch
Human dispatcher validates the alert and determines the appropriate response. AI supports — a trained human decides and dispatches the responder.

Revenue Model Overview
D1ALERT will generate revenue primarily through its services for LGUs, command centers, and private organizations, while citizens can use the emergency-reporting app for free. The main source of income will be the LGU/Command-Center License, which is priced at ₱120,000 per year, along with annual subscriptions for private-sector organizations. Additional revenue can come from one-time onboarding fees, extended training, custom integrations, specialized technical support, and organization-specific requirements. The pricing strategy follows a licensing and subscription model, allowing organizations to pay for continued access and support while keeping the citizen application free. The file does not provide a specific sales forecast, but future sales can be based on the number of LGUs, command centers, and private organizations that are successfully onboarded each year. Alternative revenue models include private-sector subscriptions and additional fees for training, integrations, and specialized support.
